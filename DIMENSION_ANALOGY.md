# My practical four-variable view

*Author's informal analogy for this notebook; not a dimensional identity or a measured limit of perception.*

**2D screen + depth cues ≈ 3D perception**

**3D view + 1 variable explored over viewing time ≈ 4-variable exploration**

**Working shorthand: 4 ≈ 2 displayed dimensions + 1 inferred depth + 1 sequential variable.**

A flat screen can convey a three-dimensional impression through shading, contour, perspective,
motion and explicit colour mappings. A slider can then reveal time or another variable sequentially.
This is the working display convention I use to organize this notebook.

Depth cues support an interpretation of geometry; they do not guarantee exact metric recovery.
Todd (2004), Figures 1–2 and the conclusion, discusses the relevant cues and ambiguity.
False colour needs a defined value mapping, while shading follows surface orientation and lighting.
Height shown through colour and height perceived through shading are related but distinct ideas.

The height-field examples retain three quantities: **x, y, h(x,y)**. Colour and shading are visual channels,
not two further independent data dimensions. In the planar colour map, h remains encoded even when the
surface has no vertical displacement.

The sequential variable may be acquisition time, a frame index, or a parameter. Viewing time is the
reader's interaction/playback time. Frame order alone does not supply verified seconds or physical speed.

**Same space + a different coordinate grid = a different organization of point support.**

This second analogy motivates comparing coordinate choices. Equal coordinate-bin widths need not
cover equal physical areas or volumes. Formal definitions, change-of-variable factors and the density/MSP
derivation belong to **Part I** and must be written as actual mathematics rather than inferred from these analogies.
